❯ what does pl.lit("missing") do?                                               
⏺ pl.lit() makes a literal: a fixed value used as a column expression.
  pl.lit("missing") means "the text missing in every row", as opposed to
  pl.col(...), which refers to an existing column.

  In the overview cell it adds a label column to each of two one-row tables
  before stacking them:

  raw.null_count().with_columns(pl.lit("missing").alias("stat"))

  - raw.null_count() returns one row: the number of nulls in each column.
  - .with_columns(pl.lit("missing").alias("stat")) adds a column named stat
    whose value is "missing". .alias("stat") gives the new column its name.

  The second table does the same with "distinct". After pl.concat stacks them,
  the stat column tells you which row is which:

  stat     | _id    | beachId | ... | eColi
  missing  | 0      | 0       | ... | 3543
  distinct | 102534 | 10      | ... | 330

  Without it you'd get two unlabelled rows of numbers. The final .select("stat",
  pl.exclude("stat")) just moves stat to the front.

⏺ Yes. Building in known differences and checking that your test finds them is a
  good use of simulation. Make some beaches the same and some different, then
  run the ANOVA on the fake data. If it flags the beaches you made different and
  not the ones you made the same, you know the method and code work before you
  use them on the real data. It also shows how big a difference the test can
  detect with this much data.

  Three things to settle before relying on the ANOVA:

  1. Use a share, not a count. The unit is one beach in one month, which works.
     But May and September have only a few sampling days each, so a raw count of
     warning days isn't comparable between months. Use the share of sampled
     days with a warning, or keep only June to August.
  2. Beaches share weather. A rainy July raises warnings at every beach at once,
     so the beaches' months aren't independent of each other. A plain one-way
     ANOVA ignores that. Instead, use a two-way ANOVA with beach and month as 
     the two factors. Month here means a specific month in a specific year, such
     as July 2014, so each beach is compared with the others in the same month.
     It's a standard extension that's easy to explain.
  3. ANOVA only says whether some beaches differ, not which ones. A follow-up
     test, usually Tukey's, compares each pair of beaches.

  Also a caution: shares of warning days are bounded between 0 and 1 and often
  sit near 0. ANOVA assumes roughly bell-shaped errors, so check the residuals.
  The simulation will show whether that's a problem.
